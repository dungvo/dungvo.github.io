## Learner sentences and natural corrections

| Learner's sentence | Natural English | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “I noticed you used present perfect tense a lot in your writting.” | “I've noticed that you use the present perfect a lot in your writing.” | Present perfect connects repeated observations to now; use **the**; spelling: **writing**. |
| “So is this tense common?” | “So, is this tense common?” | Add a comma after the discourse marker **so** in writing. |
| “I need to remember them from now on.” | “From now on, I'll try to remember these patterns.” | **From now on** points forward; **patterns** is clearer than **them**. |
| “Someone + want + someone + to + verb.” | “Someone wants someone to do something.” | A third-person singular subject takes **wants**. |
| “Sometime I noticed...” | “Sometimes I've noticed...” | **Sometimes** means on some occasions; present perfect fits a repeated observation up to now. |
| “Let's stop here because I start my work today.” | “Let's stop here because I need to start work now.” | **Need to start** expresses the immediate reason; **now** fits the moment of stopping. |
| “Enjoy vs fun vs happy.” | “What's the difference between ‘enjoy,’ ‘fun,’ and ‘happy’?” | Use the full question when asking for a distinction. |
| “People usually use enjoy when they send a courvergge to someone.” | “People often use ‘enjoy’ when they send someone a friendly wish.” | **Friendly wish** fits “Enjoy your weekend”; **encouragement** fits support, but is not normally “sent” with *enjoy*. |

## Grammar and usage map

- **How's it going?** is a casual question. **I'm doing well** is a reply.
- **It was hard at first, but now it's going well** contrasts a finished beginning with the current situation.
- **So far** means from the beginning until now. **From now on** means starting now and continuing into the future.
- Present perfect: **I've noticed**, **I've learned**, **the team has been supportive**.
- Present perfect passive: **I've been given an assignment** = someone has given me an assignment.
- **Set up** is a verb; **setup** is a noun or adjective.
- **Research the products** uses *research* as a verb; **do research on the products** uses it as a noun.
- **Want to + verb**: the subject does the action. **Want + person + to + verb**: another person does it.
- **Enjoy + noun/-ing**: *enjoy the weekend; enjoy learning*. **Have fun + -ing**: *have fun exploring*. **Happy for someone** celebrates their good news. **Happy with** means satisfied; **happy about** names the reason.

## Pronunciation focus

- **imagine** /ɪˈmædʒɪn/ — stress **MAG**; the final syllable is /dʒɪn/, not “gene.”
- **supportive** /səˈpɔːrtɪv/ — stress **PORT**.
- **assignment** /əˈsaɪnmənt/ — stress **SIGN**.
- **findings** /ˈfaɪndɪŋz/ — keep the final /z/ sound.

## Active-recall prompts

Answer aloud before checking the session cards:

1. A colleague asks, “How's it going?” Give a two-sentence answer about onboarding.
2. Contrast your difficult first day with your progress now.
3. Explain what you have learned **so far**, then state one habit you will follow **from now on**.
4. Describe your setup and documentation work using **set up**, **setup**, **go through**, and **get familiar with**.
5. Explain a manager's research assignment using the present perfect passive and **wants me to**.
6. Choose the natural response: a colleague is leaving for the weekend; a friend got a new job; you are satisfied with your environment setup.
