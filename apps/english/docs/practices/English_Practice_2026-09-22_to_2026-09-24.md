# 1. Full Workplace Situation

## Situation

A data pipeline has failed. A coworker asks you to investigate the
problem and keep them updated.

### 1. Coworker asks about the failure

**Coworker:**\
Do you know why the data pipeline failed?

**You:**\
Give me a second. I'll check it and get back to you.

### 2. You are busy and cannot investigate immediately

**Coworker:**\
Can you check why the pipeline failed?

**You:**\
I'm a bit busy right now, but I can start looking into it in about 30
minutes.

### 3. Coworker asks how long the investigation will take

**Coworker:**\
Okay. How long do you think it will take to figure out the problem?

**You:**\
I think it'll take me about two hours.

### 4. Coworker asks why you need two hours

**Coworker:**\
Why do you think it'll take two hours?

**You:**\
The problem might be complex. First, I need to check the log and
reproduce the issue. After checking the log, I need to analyze the
information before I can give you an answer.

### 5. Coworker asks for an update by 5 PM

**Coworker:**\
Can you give me an update before 5 PM?

**You:**\
I'll do my best to get back to you by 5 PM.

### 6. There is an important client meeting

**Coworker:**\
We really need the answer by 5 PM because there's a meeting with the
client.

**You:**\
I think two hours might not be enough for me to check the problem, but
I'll do my best to get back to you by 5 PM.

### 7. Coworker asks for a final deadline

**Coworker:**\
Okay. If you can't finish by 5 PM, when can you give me a final answer?

**You:**\
I can definitely get back to you with a final answer before 10 AM
tomorrow.

### 8. Coworker asks what happens if the issue is more complicated

**Coworker:**\
What if you find out that the problem is more complicated than expected?

**You:**\
If I find out that the problem is more complicated than expected and I
won't be able to give you a final answer by 10 AM tomorrow, I'll let you
know immediately.

### 9. Coworker asks you to keep them updated

**Coworker:**\
Sounds good. Please keep me updated.

**You:**\
Okay, I will.

### 10. One hour later --- you find the likely cause

You discover that a recent configuration change is related to the
pipeline failure.

**You:**\
I found out that the problem was related to a recent configuration
change. I'll check with the owner and update you once I know more.

### 11. You talk to the owner --- STOPPING POINT

**Owner:**\
Yes, I changed the configuration this morning. I can roll it back, but
it might affect another service.

**Next practice task:** Express this idea in your own English:

> Don't roll it back yet. I need to understand the impact on the other
> service first.

# 2. Core Chunks and Pronunciation

## Give me a second.

**IPA:** /ɡɪv mi ə ˈsekənd/\
**Use:** Ask someone to wait briefly.

## I'll check it.

**IPA:** /aɪl tʃek ɪt/\
**Use:** Say you will check or investigate something.

## I'll get back to you.

**IPA:** /aɪl ɡet bæk tə juː/\
**Use:** Say you will respond later after checking.

## I'm a bit busy right now.

**IPA:** /aɪm ə bɪt ˈbɪzi raɪt naʊ/\
**Use:** Politely explain that you are currently busy.

## I'll look into it.

**IPA:** /aɪl lʊk ˈɪntuː ɪt/\
**Use:** Say you will investigate something.

## It might be complex.

**IPA:** /ɪt maɪt bi ˈkɑːmpleks/\
**Use:** Express uncertainty about complexity.

## First, I need to...

**IPA:** /fɝːst aɪ niːd tə/\
**Use:** Introduce the first step in a process.

## reproduce the issue

**IPA:** /ˌriːprəˈduːs ði ˈɪʃuː/\
**Use:** Make an issue happen again so it can be investigated.

## It'll take me about...

**IPA:** /ˈɪtəl teɪk mi əˈbaʊt/\
**Use:** Estimate how much time you need.

## I'll do my best to...

**IPA:** /aɪl duː maɪ best tə/\
**Use:** Express effort without making a firm promise.

## I won't be able to...

**IPA:** /aɪ woʊnt bi ˈeɪbəl tə/\
**Use:** Say that something will not be possible.

## I found out that...

**IPA:** /aɪ faʊnd aʊt ðæt/\
**Use:** Introduce something you discovered.

## be related to...

**IPA:** /bi rɪˈleɪtɪd tə/\
**Use:** Explain that two things are connected.

**IPA:** /aɪl tʃek wɪð ði ˈoʊnər/\

## I'll update you once I know more.

**IPA:** /aɪl ʌpˈdeɪt juː wʌns aɪ noʊ mɔːr/\
**Use:** Promise another update after getting more information.

# 3. Important Sentences and Patterns

### I'm a bit busy right now, but I can start looking into it in about 30 minutes.

**Meaning:** You cannot investigate now, but you can start roughly 30
minutes from now.

**Important distinction:**\
"In about 30 minutes" here describes when you can **start**. It does not
promise that the investigation will be finished in 30 minutes.

### I think it'll take me about two hours.

**Pattern:** It'll take me + amount of time.

### The problem might be complex.

**Pattern:** might be + adjective\
Use this when you are not certain.

### First, I need to check the log and reproduce the issue.

**Pattern:** First, I need to + action.

### After checking the log, I need to analyze the information before I can give you an answer.

**Patterns:**\
- After + -ing\
- before I can + verb

### I'll do my best to get back to you by 5 PM.

**Meaning:** You will try, but you are not making an absolute promise.

### I think two hours might not be enough for me to check the problem.

**Pattern:** might not be enough for + person + to + verb.

### I can definitely get back to you with a final answer before 10 AM tomorrow.

**Meaning:** Strong confidence/certainty.

### If I find out that the problem is more complicated than expected and I won't be able to give you a final answer by 10 AM tomorrow, I'll let you know immediately.

**Pattern:** If I find out that..., I'll let you know immediately.

### I found out that the problem was related to a recent configuration change.

**Patterns:**\
- I found out that...\
- be related to...

### I'll check with the owner and update you once I know more.

**Patterns:**\
- check with + person\
- update + person\
- once I know more

# 4. Corrections to Remember

-   **can complex** → **might be complex** / **can be complex**
-   **the first, I need...** → **First, I need...**
-   **enough to me check** → **enough for me to check**
-   **related with** → **related to**
-   **check with owner** → **check with the owner**
-   **update it** (when you mean inform a person) → **update you**
-   **I find out that...** for an already completed discovery → **I
    found out that...**
-   **English** is always capitalized.
-   Capitalize the first word of a sentence.
-   Do not normally put a space before a question mark in English.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 5. Time Expressions

## by 5 PM

Means **no later than 5 PM**.\
The action can happen earlier.

## at 5 PM

Means **at that specific time**.

## in 30 minutes

Means **30 minutes from now**.

The verb/context determines what will happen then: - I'll **start
checking** in 30 minutes. - I'll **get back to you** in 30 minutes.

These have different meanings.

Another example:

**Intent:** You are uncertain about the complexity.\
Think: **uncertain → it might be...**\

# 3. Word Order: Subject, Verb, Object

Basic pattern:

**Subject + Verb + Object**

Example:

**The engineer fixed the pipeline.**

-   **The engineer** = subject
-   **fixed** = past-tense verb
-   **the pipeline** = object

Not every sentence needs an object.

Example:

**The job failed.**

-   **The job** = subject
-   **failed** = verb

# 4. make + object + base verb

The learner originally tried:

**Incorrect:** This change made job failed.\
**Incorrect:** This change made the fail job.\
**Correct:** This change made the job fail.

Pattern:

**make + object + base verb**

Examples: - The change made the job fail. - The bug made the service
crash. - The error made the application stop.

Important:

After **made + object**, use the base verb:

**made the job fail**

not:

**made the job failed**

**made** already carries the past tense.

# 5. help + person + verb

## Can you help me explain this to my client?

**IPA:** /kæn juː help mi ɪkˈspleɪn ðɪs tə maɪ ˈklaɪənt/

Pattern:

**help + person + (to) verb**

In normal conversation, the version without **to** is very common:

**help me explain**

Meaning:

The person after **help** performs or participates in the following
action.

Examples: - Can you help me fix this bug? - Can you help me understand
this code? - Can you help me prepare the presentation? - Can you help me
explain this to my client?

In:

**Can you help me explain this?**

**I** am the person who needs to explain it. The other person assists
me.

# 6. explain + something + to + someone

## Can you explain this change to me?

**IPA:** /kæn juː ɪkˈspleɪn ðɪs tʃeɪndʒ tə miː/

Pattern:

**explain + thing + to + person**

Examples: - Explain the change to me. - Explain the issue to the team. -
Explain the architecture to the client.

**Incorrect:** explain me the change\

### Can you help me explain this?

**You explain.** I receive the explanation.

# 7. I want to understand it like Tom does.

The learner wanted to express the Vietnamese idea:

**"Tôi cũng muốn hiểu giống Tom."**

Natural English:

**I want to understand it like Tom does.**

Here, **does** avoids repeating the full verb phrase.

Compare:

**Tom understands it.**

**I want to understand it like Tom does.**

In this context:

**does = understands it**

Important lesson:

Do not automatically translate Vietnamese **"cũng"** as **also**. First
identify the intended meaning. Here, the important idea is comparison
with Tom's understanding, so **like Tom does** expresses the intent more
clearly.

# 8. make vs. cause

These verbs use different patterns.

## make

Pattern:

**make + object + base verb**

Example:

**The change made the job fail.**

Not:

**made the job to fail**

## cause

Pattern:

**cause + object + to + verb**

Example:

**The change caused the job to fail.**

Not:

**caused the job fail**

Important lesson:

The verb before the object determines the structure that follows.

Compare:

**made + the job + fail**

**caused + the job + to fail**

# 9. seems to have + past participle

## The configuration change seems to have caused the pipeline to fail.

**IPA:** /ðə kənˌfɪɡjəˈreɪʃən tʃeɪndʒ siːmz tə hæv kɔːzd ðə ˈpaɪplaɪn tə
feɪl/

This is useful during technical investigation when the evidence suggests
a cause, but the root cause is not confirmed.

Compare:

### The configuration change caused the pipeline to fail.

Stronger statement. It presents the causal relationship as a fact.

### The configuration change seems to have caused the pipeline to fail.

The current evidence suggests this, but the speaker is not completely
certain.

Pattern:

**something + seems to have + past participle**

Examples: - The change seems to have caused the failure. - The service
seems to have stopped unexpectedly. - Someone seems to have changed the
configuration.

Why **seems to have caused** instead of **seems to cause**?

**seems to cause** can describe current/general behavior.

**seems to have caused** looks back at an event that already happened.

# 10. Expressing Uncertainty During Investigation

The learner's intended meaning was:

**I am still in the discovery/investigation phase, so I am not sure
whether the change caused the job to fail.**

Natural sentence:

**Because I'm still investigating, I'm not sure whether this change
caused the job to fail.**

Useful pattern:

**I'm not sure whether + sentence**

**IPA:** /aɪm nɑːt ʃʊr ˈweðər/

Avoid unnecessary double uncertainty such as:

**I think it seems...**

Usually choose one clear uncertainty structure: - **I think...** - **It
seems that...** - **seems to have...**

# 11. shortly after: Timing vs. Causation

Successful practice sentence:

**The pipeline started failing shortly after the configuration

This describes timing/correlation.

It does not by itself prove that the configuration change caused the

Expanded version:

**Can you give me an update in 30 minutes? I need to update my coworker soon.**

Important distinction:

- **a final result** = the investigation is finished
- **an update** = current information while the investigation can continue

Related earlier chunk:

**I'll update you once I know more.**

# 8. another vs. other vs. the other

## another + singular countable noun

Meaning: one more / one different.

Examples:

- another problem
- another service
- another configuration
- another option

Successful sentence:

**The configuration change fixed another problem.**

## other + plural noun

Meaning: additional/different things.

Examples:

- other problems
- other services
- other configurations
- other options

Successful sentence:

**If we roll back the configuration, other services might break.**

## the other + singular noun

Usually refers to a specific remaining/second one.

Example:

**Service A is working, but the other service is failing.**

Mental model:

- **another service** → one more/different service
- **other services** → multiple additional/different services
- **the other service** → the specific other one

---

# 9. Recommendation vs. Decision vs. Constraint

The learner noticed that the coworker's main intent was not to hear a recommendation. The coworker wanted to know why the pipeline failed and how it would be fixed.

Important distinction:

## We shouldn't roll it back.

Meaning: recommendation; rollback is probably a bad idea.

## We won't roll it back.

Meaning: decision or plan.

## We can't roll it back for now.

Meaning: current constraint; rollback is not currently a viable option.

Important communication lesson:

Choose the sentence based not only on grammar, but also on the speaker's intent and what the listener needs to know.

# 10. Dependency and Next-Step Language

Successful sentence:

**We need to update the service that still depends on the old routing first.**

Useful structure:

**the service that still depends on...**

`that` identifies which service is being discussed.

Successful sentence:

**After that, we can roll back the configuration and check whether the pipeline works correctly.**

Useful pattern:

**check whether + subject + verb**

Compare:

- **check the pipeline** → inspect the pipeline
- **check whether the pipeline works correctly** → verify if it works correctly

# 11. Passive Voice with might

Initial attempts:

**I think the issue might fix in two days.**

**The issue might fixed in two days.**

Correct sentence:

**So, I think the issue might be fixed in two days.**

Pattern:

**might + be + past participle**

Compare:

**Tom might fix the issue.** → active

**The issue might be fixed.** → passive

Time nuance:

- **in two days** → roughly two days from now / may take two days
- **within two days** → no later than two days from now

- **The configuration change make the pipeline fail.** → **The configuration change made the pipeline fail.**
- **Can you explain data flow to me understand?** → **Can you explain the data flow to me so that I can understand...**
- **why you change configuration** → **why you changed the configuration**
- **when we rolling back could fix...** → **rolling back could fix...**
- **the next step we should check...** → **the next step is to check...**
- **other service** when referring to several services → **other services**
- **Tom need to update...** → **Tom needs to update...**
- **I think the issue might fix...** → **I think the issue might be fixed...**

# Daily-Life Reading Practice — The Birthday Dinner That Almost Went Wrong

## Purpose

Practice previously learned chunks in a non-work situation. Read the story one section at a time, explain the meaning in your own English, and answer the comprehension questions using the suggested chunks.

## Story

On Saturday morning, Mia was preparing a birthday dinner for her mother. She had invited twelve family members and reserved a private room at a small Italian restaurant. She wanted everything to be perfect because her mother rarely allowed the family to organize anything special for her.

Shortly after Mia finished decorating the birthday cake, her brother Daniel called her.

“I noticed that the weather is getting worse,” Daniel said. “It might rain heavily this evening.”

“I saw the forecast too,” Mia replied. “I’ll look into it and get back to you. The restaurant has an indoor room, so I don’t think the rain will be a serious problem.”

Daniel was silent for a moment. Then he said, “There’s another problem. Dad’s car won’t start.”

“What happened?”

“I’m not sure. It was working yesterday, but it stopped working shortly after Dad filled the tank this morning. The new fuel seems to have caused the problem.”

Mia didn’t want to reach a conclusion too quickly. “The timing is suspicious, but that doesn’t necessarily mean the fuel caused the car to stop working. Can you ask the mechanic to check it?”

“I already called him,” Daniel said. “He’s a bit busy right now, but he can start looking into it in about thirty minutes.”

“Great. Please update me once you know more.”

After the call, Mia continued preparing the cake. However, the conversation had made her feel nervous. Her father was supposed to pick up her grandmother and two other relatives. If his car was not available, they would need another driver.

A few minutes later, Mia’s cousin Lily arrived at the house.

“You look worried,” Lily said. “Can I help you with anything?”

“Yes,” Mia replied. “Can you help me move these decorations into the car? After that, can you check whether we have enough candles?”

Lily counted the candles and found only six.

“We need another pack,” she said.

“Are you sure? I bought some yesterday.”

“There are six candles in this box. The other box is empty.”

“If I understand correctly, we have enough decorations but not enough candles. Is that right?”

“Exactly.”

“The next step is to buy another pack before we go to the restaurant,” Mia said. “But we can’t leave yet because I’m still waiting for an update from Daniel.”

At eleven o’clock, Daniel called again.

“The mechanic found out that the battery was almost dead,” he explained. “The fuel didn’t cause the problem. The cold weather made the battery lose power more quickly.”

“So the car might be fixed soon?”

“Yes, but the mechanic needs to replace the battery. He thinks it’ll take about two hours.”

“Two hours sounds like a long time. Why does he think it’ll take two hours?”

“He has to finish another repair first. Then he needs to get the correct battery from a store on the other side of town.”

Mia looked at the clock. The dinner would begin at six.

“Can he definitely fix it by four?” she asked.

“He said he’ll do his best, but he can’t promise.”

“Okay. We shouldn’t depend entirely on Dad’s car. I’ll ask Aunt Sarah whether she can pick up Grandma.”

Mia called her aunt and explained the situation to her.

“Can you pick up Grandma this afternoon?” Mia asked. “Dad’s car might not be fixed in time.”

“I can pick her up,” Sarah replied, “but my son has a football game. I won’t be able to leave before four thirty.”

“That should be fine. I’ll send you Grandma’s address so that you can find her house easily.”

“Please explain the plan to Grandma too,” Sarah said. “She sometimes gets confused when plans change suddenly.”

“Of course. I’ll call her now.”

When Mia called her grandmother, she spoke slowly.

“Dad’s car has a battery problem, so Aunt Sarah will pick you up instead. She’ll arrive at about five.”

Her grandmother asked, “Does that mean your father isn’t coming to dinner?”

“No, he’s still coming. He just won’t be able to drive you.”

“So, if I understand correctly, Sarah will pick me up, and your father will come in another car.”

“That’s right.”

“Good. Please keep me updated if anything changes.”

At two in the afternoon, the restaurant manager called.

“I’m sorry,” he said, “but a pipe in our private room started leaking this morning. The leak seems to have damaged part of the ceiling.”

Mia took a deep breath. “Can you explain the situation to me? Is the room still safe?”

“We’re still investigating, but we can’t use that room for now. We have another room upstairs, although it’s smaller.”

“How many people can it hold?”

“It can hold ten people comfortably. You have twelve guests, don’t you?”

“Yes. Could you help me understand our other options?”

The manager thought for a moment. “We could put another table near the door, but it might block the walkway. The other option is to move your dinner to seven thirty, when the main dining room will be less crowded.”

Mia considered both choices. Adding a table could make everyone uncomfortable, but changing the time would affect all the guests.

“We shouldn’t put a table near the door if it creates a safety problem,” she said. “And we can’t change the time without speaking to everyone first. I’ll check with my family and get back to you by three.”

Mia created a family group call. She explained the problem and asked everyone what they preferred.

Her uncle said, “I think we should move the dinner to seven thirty. That will give Dad’s mechanic more time too.”

Lily disagreed. “Grandma doesn’t like eating late. Could we choose another restaurant?”

“That could be difficult,” Mia said. “Most restaurants depend on weekend reservations, so they might not have space for twelve people at short notice.”

Daniel suggested using Mia’s house instead.

“We already have the cake and decorations here,” he said. “We could order food from the restaurant and have dinner at home.”

“That might work,” Mia said. “But first, I need to check whether we have enough chairs and plates.”

After counting everything, Mia discovered that they had enough plates but needed four more chairs. Daniel offered to bring two chairs, and Aunt Sarah said she could bring the other two.

“So, if I understand correctly,” Mia said, “everyone prefers to have dinner here. Daniel and Sarah will bring the extra chairs, and I’ll ask the restaurant to deliver the food. Is that right?”

Everyone agreed.

Mia called the restaurant manager again.

“We won’t use the private room,” she told him. “We’ve decided to have the dinner at home. Can you deliver our food at six thirty?”

“I think so,” he replied. “Give me a second. I’ll check with the kitchen.”

A minute later, he returned.

“Yes, we can deliver it by six thirty.”

“Perfect. Please update me if there are any delays.”

By five thirty, the house was ready. The mechanic had replaced the battery, Aunt Sarah had picked up Grandma, and all the extra chairs had arrived. The rain was heavy, but everyone was safe inside.

Mia’s mother entered the house and looked around in surprise.

“I thought we were going to a restaurant,” she said.

“That was the original plan,” Mia replied. “But several things went wrong. Dad’s car broke down, and a leaking pipe made the restaurant’s private room unusable.”

“That sounds stressful.”

“It was, but everyone helped. The problems made us change the plan, but they didn’t cause us to cancel the celebration.”

Her mother smiled. “Sometimes another plan becomes a better plan.”

Shortly after six thirty, the food arrived. Everyone sat around the tables, shared stories, and laughed about the day’s problems. The celebration was not perfect, but it felt warm and personal.

At the end of the evening, Mia realized something important: a good plan matters, but being able to explain problems, confirm your understanding, and communicate the next step matters even more.

## Chunk Guide

### Investigating and updating

- **I’ll look into it.** = I will investigate it.
- **I’ll get back to you.** = I will contact you again after checking.
- **Please update me once you know more.** = Tell me when you receive more information.
- **I found out that...** = Introduces information that you discovered.

### Observation, timing, and cause

- **I noticed that...** introduces an observation.
- **shortly after + event** describes timing, but does not prove causation.
- **seems to have + past participle** expresses a likely but unconfirmed past event.
- **make + object + base verb**: The cold weather made the battery lose power.
- **cause + object + to + verb**: The problem caused us to change the plan.
- **make + object + adjective**: The leak made the room unusable.

### Explaining and understanding

- **explain + thing + to + person**: Explain the situation to me.
- **help + person + base verb**: Help me understand the options.
- **so that + subject + can + verb** explains purpose.
- **If I understand correctly, ... Is that right?** checks your understanding.

### Plans and choices

- **The next step is to + verb** introduces the next action.
- **After that, we can...** introduces a later action.
- **check whether + subject + verb** means verify whether something is true.
- **another + singular noun**: another pack.
- **other + plural noun**: other options.
- **the other + singular noun**: the other box.

### Recommendation, decision, and constraint

- **We shouldn’t...** expresses a recommendation.
- **We won’t...** expresses a decision.
- **We can’t... for now** expresses a current constraint.
