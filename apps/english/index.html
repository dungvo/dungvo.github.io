HOW TO STUDY

Start with meaning, not translation.

For each lesson, imagine the situation, decide what you want the listener to understand, then choose a complete English chunk. Read the dialogue aloud, answer the exercises, and reuse the chunks in a new situation.

  1. Situation — understand the real context.
  2. Intent — decide what you want to achieve.
  3. Chunks — learn complete reusable phrases.
  4. Speak — answer aloud before checking examples.
01

WORKPLACE COMMUNICATION

Clarifying a workplace request

Situation: You receive a vague task in an employee portal. You want to confirm the correct date and document without sounding careless.

Your intent

  • Show that you read the request.
  • Identify the exact unclear point.
  • Confirm the expected action before proceeding.

Natural chunks

Just to make sure I understand, …Check your interpretation.

Could you clarify what you mean by …?Ask about ambiguous wording.

Am I right in thinking that …?Confirm an assumption politely.

Should I use X, or would you prefer Y?Present two clear options.

Dialogue

Maya: Could you update your personal information in the employee portal?

Daniel: Sure. Just to make sure I understand, should I enter the date when the information became valid, or today's date?

Maya: Use the date when it became valid.

Daniel: Got it. Could you clarify which supporting document is required?

Maya: A current identification document is fine.

Why it sounds natural

“Just to make sure I understand” is collaborative. It is softer than “Your instructions are unclear.” A precise either-or question is also easier to answer.

Practice

  1. Complete: “Just to make sure I ___, the deadline is Friday, correct?”
  2. Make this natural: “Tell me what document you need.”
  3. Speak: Your manager says, “Update the report soon.” Clarify the deadline, format, and audience.
02

DECISION-MAKING

Making a decision without rushing

Situation: One solution is faster to build; another is easier to maintain. Guide the team toward a sound decision.

Your intent

  • Compare the options fairly.
  • Name the main trade-off.
  • State a careful preference and propose one more check.

Natural chunks

We need to weigh X against Y.Compare competing priorities.

The main trade-off is …Name the central compromise.

It depends on what we're optimizing for.Connect the choice to the goal.

Given what we know, I'd lean toward …State a careful preference.

Dialogue

Lena: Option A could be ready next week. Option B will take longer.

Omar: True, but we need to weigh delivery speed against long-term maintenance.

Lena: Which one do you prefer?

Omar: It depends on what we're optimizing for. For a short experiment, I'd lean toward A. For a core service, B may be safer.

Lena: That makes sense.

Omar: Before we commit, I'd like to estimate the maintenance cost of both options.

Why it sounds natural

“I'd lean toward” gives a preference without pretending the decision is final. If you say “It depends,” immediately explain the relevant condition.

Practice

  1. Compare a cloud service with a self-hosted system using “The main trade-off is…”
  2. Finish: “Given what we know, I'd lean toward ___ because ___.”
  3. Speak: Choose between a basic feature this month and a complete feature next quarter.
03

RISK & PROBLEM-SOLVING

Raising a risk and proposing a solution

Situation: A service passed basic tests, but its behavior under heavy traffic is unknown.

Your intent

  • Acknowledge progress.
  • Describe the risk and impact calmly.
  • Suggest a practical mitigation.

Natural chunks

That looks promising, but I'm concerned about …Balance encouragement with caution.

We haven't fully validated … yet.Identify an unfinished check.

The risk is that …Explain a possible negative outcome.

We can reduce the risk by …Propose mitigation.

Dialogue

Priya: The service passed the basic tests. Can we release it tomorrow?

Noah: That looks promising, but I'm concerned about performance under heavy traffic.

Priya: What could happen?

Noah: The risk is that response times could increase sharply. We haven't fully validated the scaling behavior yet.

Priya: What do you suggest?

Noah: We can run a load test and release to a small group first. If the results are consistent, we can move forward.

Useful pattern

A strong risk statement has three parts: risk → impact → mitigation. Avoid vague warnings when you can explain the concrete consequence.

Practice

  1. A data source arrives late. Explain the dashboard impact and propose a freshness check.
  2. Soften: “We cannot release this. It will fail.”
  3. Speak: Raise one risk and suggest two safeguards for a database migration.
04

DATA SCIENCE & ML

Explaining an ML recommendation

Situation: Offline results look good, but a stakeholder wants an immediate full deployment.

Your intent

  • Separate test performance from production performance.
  • Explain uncertainty in business-friendly language.
  • Recommend a measurable next step.

Natural chunks

The early results are encouraging.Acknowledge positive evidence.

That doesn't necessarily mean …Prevent an incorrect conclusion.

We need to validate the model against …Request a relevant evaluation.

I'd recommend a limited rollout so that we can …Propose a controlled experiment.

Dialogue

Business lead: The accuracy looks high. Are we ready to use the model for every customer?

Data scientist: The early results are encouraging, but that doesn't necessarily mean performance will be stable in production.

Business lead: What's still uncertain?

Data scientist: We need to validate it against recent data and check different user groups. I'd watch the false-positive rate closely.

Business lead: What do you recommend?

Data scientist: A limited rollout, so we can measure real-world impact before expanding.

Make metrics meaningful

Connect every metric to an outcome: “False positives matter because unnecessary alerts create extra work for the operations team.”

Practice

  1. Explain data drift, false positives, and A/B testing without jargon.
  2. Complete: “Latency increased. This matters because…”
  3. Speak: Explain why a forecast for common products may not work for new products.
05

LEADERSHIP

Giving feedback and managing expectations

Situation: An engineer delivered a strong fix, but another team learned about a schema change too late.

Your intent

  • Recognize the contribution.
  • Describe observable behavior and impact.
  • Agree on a better next-time behavior.

Natural chunks

I appreciate the work you put into …Recognize effort.

One thing that could have gone better is …Introduce improvement gently.

The impact was that …Connect behavior to consequences.

Next time, could you …?Request a specific behavior.

Dialogue

Manager: I appreciate the work you put into stabilizing the pipeline. The technical fix was strong.

Engineer: Was there anything I could improve?

Manager: One thing that could have gone better is communication around the schema change. The analytics team had very little time to adjust.

Engineer: I should have told them earlier.

Manager: Exactly. Next time, could you share the proposed change before implementation?

Useful pattern

Use behavior → impact → next step. “The update came after implementation” is more useful than “You are not communicative.”

Practice

  1. Replace “You are careless” with an observable behavior.
  2. Say that a request is possible, but not by tomorrow.
  3. Speak: Give feedback to a teammate who solved an incident but did not document it.
06

PROFESSIONAL ETHICS

Handling an ethical concern professionally

Situation: Someone proposes using personal data for an analysis, but the purpose and approval are unclear.

Your intent

  • Identify the concern neutrally.
  • Check necessity and purpose.
  • Request appropriate review without accusing anyone.

Natural chunks

Before we proceed, we should confirm …Pause for an important check.

I'm not sure this use is consistent with …Raise a possible mismatch.

Do we have a clear reason to access …?Test necessity.

Let's ask the appropriate team to review this.Escalate constructively.

Dialogue

Colleague: We could combine profile data with browsing behavior to improve the campaign.

You: Before we proceed, we should confirm that this use matches the purpose for which the data was collected.

Colleague: It's only an experiment.

You: I understand, but we still need a clear reason to access it and should be transparent about the analysis.

Colleague: Should we stop the project?

You: Let's pause this part and ask the appropriate team to review it. We can continue with aggregated data meanwhile.

Keep it constructive

Focus on the proposed action and required check—not on the person's motives. “Before we proceed…” creates space for review.

Practice

  1. Neutralize: “This is unethical and you shouldn't do it.”
  2. Ask questions about purpose, access, and transparency.
  3. Speak: Respond to a request to hide a known limitation from a project update.
07

COMPENSATION & BENEFITS

Asking about an offer clearly

Situation: A fictional offer includes base pay, a possible performance award, leave, and health benefits.

Your intent

  • Separate guaranteed and variable compensation.
  • Clarify eligibility, calculation, and timing.
  • Avoid assumptions based on examples.

Natural chunks

Could you walk me through how … works?Request a clear explanation.

Is this guaranteed, or does it depend on …?Separate fixed and conditional items.

How is the amount determined?Ask about calculation.

When would I become eligible for …?Clarify eligibility timing.

Dialogue

Candidate: Could you walk me through how the performance award works?

Recruiter: It's variable and depends on company and individual performance.

Candidate: Is any part guaranteed, or is the full amount performance-based?

Recruiter: The full amount is performance-based.

Candidate: When would I become eligible, and how is the amount determined for someone who joins during the year?

Recruiter: I'll send you the general guidelines.

Stay precise

Separate base pay, variable pay, and benefits. “Could you walk me through…?” invites a structured explanation without sounding demanding.

Practice

  1. Write one question each about eligibility, calculation, timing, and coverage.
  2. Improve: “How much bonus will I definitely get?”
  3. Speak: Ask about leave, health coverage, remote-work support, and variable pay.
08

EVERYDAY LIFE

Handling an unexpected change

Situation: An urgent family matter means you need to change plans with a friend.

Your intent

  • Explain that circumstances changed.
  • Apologize without over-explaining.
  • Offer a concrete alternative.

Natural chunks

Something unexpected came up.Introduce an unforeseen problem.

I'm afraid I won't be able to …Give disappointing news politely.

I'm sorry for the short notice.Acknowledge the timing.

Would it work for you if we … instead?Suggest an alternative.

Dialogue

Minh: Something unexpected came up with my family. I'm afraid I won't be able to make dinner tonight.

Sara: No problem. Is everything okay?

Minh: Yes, but I need to help with something. I'm sorry for the short notice. Would Saturday work instead?

Sara: Saturday works for me.

Minh: Great—thanks for understanding.

Protect your privacy

“Something came up” is natural when you do not want to share private details. A specific alternative shows that you still value the plan.

Practice

  1. Use the same chunks to reschedule an appointment and a work call.
  2. Say the message once in a friendly tone and once professionally.
  3. Speak: Leave a 30-second message with a brief reason and two alternatives.

FINAL INTEGRATION

A difficult project update

Your ML feature has promising offline results, but recent data is unchecked. A stakeholder wants a release this week. Communication with another team was late, and one sensitive field may not be necessary.

Give a two-minute update: acknowledge progress, clarify the priority, explain one risk and its impact, propose a limited rollout, raise the data-use concern neutrally, and set a communication expectation.

The early results are encouraging. Just to make sure I understand, are we optimizing for ___ or ___? I'm concerned about ___ because ___. We can reduce the risk by ___. Before we proceed, we should confirm ___. Next time, could we ___?

Self-check

  • Did I state my intent clearly?
  • Did I use complete chunks instead of translating word by word?
  • Did I connect technical details to impact?
  • Did I propose a next step?
